what I keep

A short, honest note about your data

Galaxies needs to know who you are so it can tell your empire apart from six other people's empires. That is the whole reason any of this is stored. Everything below is what that costs you, said plainly.

Last updated 20 July 2026

Sign-in is Google only, and here is everything I hold

There is no Galaxies password, because I never wanted to be the person holding one. You sign in with a Google account, Google confirms it is you, and from there the data falls into three piles: what Google hands me, what you make by playing, and what the service records on its own. All three are below. Listing only the first would be the flattering version rather than the true one.

What Google hands me when you sign in

Google subject id
The stable identifier Google gives me for your account. It is the key your empires hang off. It is not your password and it cannot be used to sign in to anything else.
Email address
Used to reach you about the game: turn deadlines, a game starting, a game ending, and security notices. It is not sold, not rented, and not put on a marketing list.
Display name
The name other players see in the lobby and the score table. You can change it, and it does not have to be your real name.
Avatar url
A link to the picture on your Google account, shown next to your name in the lobby and on profiles. It is a link to Google's copy, not a copy I make.
Email verified flag
Whether Google considers that address verified. I require a verified one, because it is the cheapest floor there is against throwaway accounts and scripted abuse.

I do not receive your Google password, your contacts, your Drive, or your calendar. The sign-in scopes I ask for are basic profile and email, and nothing more.

What you make by playing

Game membership
Which games you are in, which empire slot is yours in each, whether you are a player or the host, and when you joined.
Orders, turn files, and your intel
The orders you submit, the turn files they produce, and the private fog-of-war view the engine writes for your empire each turn. A turn-based strategy game cannot resolve a turn without these, and your intel is only ever served to you.
Messages and names
In-game messages you send, and the empire and race names you choose. Messages are delivered to their recipients, and kept for moderation if somebody reports one.
Turn clock and history
Submission and deadline timestamps, missed-turn counts, and the final standings of games you finish. This is what decides when a turn generates and who everyone else is waiting on.

What the service records on its own

Request logs
Timestamp, IP address, request path, response status, user agent, and, when you are signed in, your account id. This is how rate limiting, abuse defence, and failure diagnosis work. Sensitive fields are kept out of the logs.
Session and refresh token records
What keeps you signed in, and what lets you sign out everywhere.
Error reports
Crash reports from the client and the server. They can include a stack trace and a game and turn identifier.
Audit events
Sign-ins, account changes, moderation actions, suspensions. These outlive a deleted account in anonymised form, so an abuse history cannot be cleared by making a new account.
Product analytics, if it is switched on
Which parts of the service people actually use. Whether this runs at launch, and which product it would use, is not settled yet.

Anything you write to me (support email, a bug report, an appeal) I keep as well, so I can handle it and refer back to it. If a feature ever needs a category that is not on this page, the page changes before the feature ships.

Ads, and the consent step for EU and UK visitors

Galaxies is ad-supported. Ads appear on this marketing site, the game browser and lobby, profile pages, and the game-over summary. They never appear in an active game (the map, your orders, combat), never on an error page, never on the account-deletion flow, and they never autoplay audio.

Ads come from a third-party ad network, and like every ad network it sets its own cookies and device identifiers to count impressions and, if you allow it, to personalise what you see. That data goes to the ad network on its own terms, not to me. I do not hand it your email, your display name, or anything about your games.

If you are in the EU, the UK, the EEA, or Switzerland, you get asked before any personalised advertising happens. The consent platform that does the asking is not wired up yet, and until it is, no ad script runs at all. When it ships it will ask you first, record your answer, and let you say no and keep playing (you will simply see less relevant ads).

The control that reopens that choice and changes it later comes with the consent platform, so it is not on this site today. There is no privacy settings link in the footer yet, and I would rather say that than point you at a link that does not exist. Until it is live, email coop@farehard.com and I will handle it by hand.

What I never do

  • I do not sell or rent your email address, your identity, or your game data.
  • I never pass your account details to a data broker, and I do not run a data-broker relationship of any kind.
  • I never read your private orders for any purpose other than generating the turn you asked for.
  • I never use your data to build a profile of you for anyone outside the game.

Note

The ad network is a separate company with its own privacy terms. I can tell you truthfully what I do not do; I cannot promise you what a third party does inside its own systems, so I will not pretend otherwise.

One honest wrinkle on that first line. Serving ads means the ad network receives ad-serving signals under whatever consent choice you make. Whether that counts as a "sale" or a "share" under some US state privacy laws is a question I have put to a lawyer and have not had answered yet. I would rather flag it than write "never sell" in bold and hope nobody checks the definition.

Getting your data out

The self-serve export button is not built yet. When it lands, you will be able to request an export bundle (a data subject access request, if you want the formal name for it) from your account settings, and it will be assembled for you to download as a machine-readable archive.

Until then, email coop@farehard.com and ask for your data. I put the bundle together by hand and send you a link. It is slower than a button, and it is a real answer rather than a promised one.

Either way the bundle contains your account record, your game membership, your saved orders and turn files for games you are in, and any messages you sent through the game. Ask for it as often as you need it; there is no charge and no form to argue with.

Two limits worth knowing before you ask. While a game is still running you get the current turn's intel view rather than every historical turn, because per-empire intel is regenerated every turn and the full history is large; once the game finishes you can export the whole run for your empire. And the bundle is yours only. Your intel contains what your empire has seen of other empires, and that comes with it, but I will not hand over another player's orders, their account details, or their messages to somebody else.

Deleting your account

Deletion will be self-serve. It is not yet, so today it runs through email: write to coop@farehard.com and say you want your account deleted. You do not have to explain yourself, and you will not be talked out of it. You will have to wait on a reply, because one person reads that mailbox.

Here is the honest part, because it matters and most services skip it. Deleting your account does not corrupt other players' live games. A game running for weeks cannot survive an empire being cut out of history, so your account record and your identity go, and your empires stay in play in one of two ways: anonymised, so the empire keeps running under a neutral name with nothing tying it to you, or handed to an AI seat that finishes out the game.

What other players already saw stays where it is. Turn reports, battle logs, and messages you sent to other empires live in their game history too, and I cannot reach into their copies to unsay things. Your name comes off; the events of the game remain the events of the game.

What this note is not

This is a summary, written to be read by a player in three minutes. It is not the full policy, and calling it one would be a lie.

The full privacy policy is written, and it is the document that governs. Where this page and the full policy disagree, the full policy wins. It goes into the detail a summary cannot carry: the legal basis for each use under GDPR and the UK GDPR, every third party by name with what it receives and what role it plays, retention schedules in actual days, the security posture in specifics, where the data is processed and on what transfer mechanism, and the minimum age of 16 and how it is handled.

It is not published on this site yet, for a boring and honest reason: it is a draft, it has not been through a lawyer, and it says so at the top. I am not going to put an unreviewed legal document up as though it were in force. It gets published here when counsel has been through it, before sign-ups open. Until then, ask me anything on it at coop@farehard.com and you will get the real answer.

Genuinely undecided, as of today:

  • Whether the ad network's behaviour counts as a "sale" or a "share" under the CCPA and CPRA, and whether this site therefore needs a "Do Not Sell or Share My Personal Information" link. This one is with a lawyer.
  • Whether a data protection officer, an EU representative, or a UK representative is required. None is appointed today.
  • Which Google Cloud region the service runs in, which decides the transfer story.
  • Which transactional email provider sends the deadline reminders.
  • Whether product analytics is switched on at launch, and which product it would be.
  • How long backups hold a deleted account before they roll over. There is a backup cycle; the exact window is not pinned down.

Those get answered as they become real rather than promised in advance. If you need an answer that is not on this page, ask, and you will get the true answer including "not decided yet".

Questions about your data go to coop@farehard.com.
